# Seven markets, both directions

Most payment providers describe themselves by the currencies they *accept*. Coinomad describes itself by the markets it operates — and the direction money can flow.

Right now that's seven markets: **NGN · USD · EUR · GBP · CAD · MXN · BRL**.

The key phrase isn't "seven markets." It's **"both directions."**

## What "both directions" actually means

A one-directional rail moves value in, or moves value out. You can collect dollars in the US, or you can pay naira out to Nigeria — but not both on the same infrastructure, and rarely with conversion in the middle.

A two-directional market means value can enter *and* leave each of those seven markets, converted on the way. Collect in Lagos, hold in USD, pay out in Mexico City — one settlement layer, no seven local banking relationships.

That's the difference between a payment processor and settlement infrastructure. Processors patch over the rails. We are the rails.

## The rails behind the markets

Each market is reachable through the local clearing system people actually use:

- **USD** — ACH
- **EUR** — SEPA
- **GBP** — SWIFT / local clearing
- **NGN** — local NGN rails
- **BRL** — PIX
- **MXN** — SPEI
- **CAD** — Interac

That local-clearing last mile is what turns a fast stablecoin settlement into money a recipient can actually spend — because settlement speed is worthless if it stalls at the destination.

## Beyond the seven

The seven markets are the live, client-facing footprint. Around them sit two wider layers:

- **30+ countries** for FX, extending reach beyond the core rails.
- **13+ networks** for stablecoin movement — Base, Polygon, Solana, BSC, Tron, TON, XRP, Ethereum, Bitcoin, Bitcoin Cash, Dogecoin, Avalanche, Litecoin, and Arbitrum — so value can move on whichever network a counterparty already uses.

## What it enables in practice

The point of "seven markets, both directions" isn't the number. It's what a treasury team can do without leaving the platform:

- **Cross-border payroll** — one payout run that lands in seven currencies, not seven wire transfers.
- **B2B supplier settlement** — pay a supplier abroad and convert on the way, without the correspondent chain.
- **Marketplace and platform payouts** — move money in one market and disburse it in another, programmatically.

For an operator running in several regions — an iGaming business, a payroll platform, a global merchant — the value is that these stop being seven separate problems. They become one settlement layer.
